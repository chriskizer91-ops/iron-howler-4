## For ChatGPT

You're making reference art for **Ironhowl 4**, a mech shooter. Each item gets two things:

1. **A character sheet**, a design page you create first. Design every item on it.
2. **HD view images** made from that sheet: front, side and back as separate images (a top view as well where listed).
   Use the character sheet as your reference, so the HD views match it exactly.

Claude builds 3D models directly from the HD views. It traces their outlines to build the model, then prints the views onto
it as the model's paint. So the HD views are measurements, not illustrations. Please follow the rules for HD views exactly.

### Rules for every HD view
- **True orthographic** view, as in a technical drawing. No perspective at all, no lens distortion, no tilt. The camera is exactly
  level and square-on: dead front, dead side or dead back (or straight down for a top view).
- **Same object, same scale, same position in all of its views.** The lowest point sits on the same pixel row in every
  view, and so does the highest point. Centre the object left to right. Don't zoom in or out between views.
- The whole object is in the frame with a margin of about 5% on every side. Nothing is cut off.
- **Plain, flat, light-grey background** (about #C8C8CA). No floor, no ground shadow, no reflection, no text, labels,
  numbers in the margin, border, watermark or other objects.
- **Soft, even, shadowless light**, like a bright overcast day, with the same light in every view. Keep highlights small
  and use no rim light or dramatic shading. These pictures become the model's paint, so any lighting gets painted on.
  Lights that glow (eyes, lamps) can glow, but with no halo spreading onto the background.
- The same paint, wear, numbers and decals in every view, on the same parts.
- The left and right halves mirror each other unless the item notes say otherwise.
- **Readable, separate parts.** Leave clear gaps between parts. Nothing hair-thin (anything long and thin, like an antenna, is
  at least 5 cm across), nothing see-through, no smoke, sparks, motion blur, muzzle flash or battle damage beyond worn paint.
- The pose is the one described for that item, the same in every view.
- Put the real size of each item in metres in the README (see the end). It isn't drawn in the picture.

### Rules for every character sheet
A clean design page (landscape 1536 × 1024) for each item or family below. Include:
- a three-quarter hero view;
- small front, side and back views;
- close-ups of the important details;
- a size bar in metres;
- each item's name.

Where it says so, add a small inset of the Wraith wearing the kit, to scale. Design here first, then make the HD views from it.

### The Wraith (for scale and style; already built, don't redesign it)
The attached pictures show the Wraith from the front, side and back. It's a 6.6 m tall recon mech with:
- orange armour plates with chipped, worn edges and white diagonal stripes;
- a charcoal and gunmetal frame;
- amber glowing lights and visor;
- white number 09 stencils;
- reverse-bent legs and clawed feet.

All of its kit follows this style: worn orange armour on a charcoal and gunmetal frame, white diagonal stripes, small white stencil codes,
amber lights and a small white wolf-head emblem. The kit is interchangeable, so every forearm weapon fits either forearm, and
so on.

Where kit fits on the Wraith:
- **Forearm saddle (both arms):** each weapon clamps onto the outer side of a forearm, about 1.4 m long, and points
  forward along it, past the fist.
- **Shoulder socket (both shoulders):** a flat round socket on top of each shoulder pad, about 0.75 m across. A module
  stands on it on a short swivel base and faces forward.
- **Back dock:** a flat rectangular plate on the back, about 1.5 m wide and 1.5 m tall. A back module bolts flat against it.
- **Drone dock:** on top of the back module; the drone sits there when it isn't flying.
