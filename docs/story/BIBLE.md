# Ironhowl 4 — story bible ("Always Do"), chosen 8 Nov 2026

Merged by the lead (Claude) from three pitches in this folder: the heart (pitch B: people, Rook's "Bring it home",
the pilot Jo and the Calloways), the mystery (pitch A: the Lazy Seven brand, fair clues, FORGE's never-cancelled order,
"Birdie") and the world (pitch C: embers and quench, Vane Light & Quench, wreck farms, the painted look). Where they
disagree, this file wins. Read the pitches for texture; don't reintroduce what this file dropped (Silas Vane as the voice,
Hal Ostrander's alias, Tess, Ez Pike, Castellan, red lamp relays, Unit 09 as a villain).

## Logline
2176. A farm kid in a dug-up 2126 mech guards the families digging FORGE's wrecks out of their own fields. The voice
waking the machines is Howl, the hero who broke FORGE in 2126, and Rook, his old handler, has believed for fifty years
that she sent him to die.

## The world in 2176 (use freely in pictures and lines)
- **The Seal ended this spring.** After 2126 FORGE's zones were fenced for fifty years. Now they're open, and the
  **salvage rush** is on: families and outfits dig the old war zones for cores.
- **Embers.** Every FORGE machine holds a core of red glass the size of a melon, still warm after fifty years. Crews
  **quench** it in blue brine barrels (it hisses into steam) and it turns grey: a battery that heats a farmhouse for a
  winter. Selling a live red core is illegal. Kids chant **"Red eye, run. Grey eye, done."**
- **Vane Light & Quench**, the biggest outfit, run by **Silas Vane** (60s, bone-white coat, one white glove, soft voice).
  It strips claims with heavy diggers, buys emptied fields cheap, and quietly pays triple for live red cores. Last
  winter its diggers bulldozed the old Howell farmhouse. He is a vulture and the obvious suspect — **not the voice.**
- **Wreck farms:** wheat grows through Tank treads; the Calloway farmhouse leans against a fallen Thresher's claw; on
  frosty mornings old wrecks "tick". Crews camp under tarps between a dead Gunner's ribs and fly their crew number on a
  flag on a whip pole.
- **Harbor 9** is a boom town: a chalk price board (HOUND 40 · GUNNER 300 · LIVE: ASK AT VANE), cranes, core crates.
- **The pine woods** grew back taller than a Gunner, up through mossy wrecks.
- **Foundry 4** was guarded until this spring. FORGE's eye is still in its tower, cracked in a star. A plaque at the
  gate: UNITS 01–07 · 2126 · THEY HELD THE LINE.

## What really happened (the truth, revealed slowly)
- **2126.** Rook (Ada Rourke, then 24) handled seven pilots from a bunker; six died. At Foundry 4 she said "go", and
  Howl (Daniel "Danny" Howell, Unit 07, Howler-07) broke FORGE's eye. The signal died. Howler-07 lay in the slag; Howl was
  listed missing, presumed dead. Their call and answer, every mission: **"Bring it home." — "Always do."** He called her
  **Birdie** (a rook is a bird); nobody else ever did.
- **He walked out**, burned, with a cracked piece of the eye so nobody could rebuild it, and went home to the Howell farm
  across the creek from the Calloways. "The war had my name. I wanted it back." He let everyone believe he was dead,
  Rook too. He married June; she is buried at the end of his field. The Howell cattle brand is a **seven lying on its
  side, the "Lazy Seven"** (turned upright it's a 7: Unit 07).
- **2176.** The Seal opens. Vane's diggers bulldoze the Howell house and head for June's grave. Howl, 76, walks back into
  Foundry 4 the way he came out, fits the piece back into the eye, and gives it one order: **guard the fields; let no one
  take from them.** The eye can't decide any more, but it can still obey and design: it wakes the 2126 wrecks and builds
  new machines (the Alpha Hound, Gunner Prime, the Brood Skitter). Howl signs every order with the Lazy Seven. He speaks
  through the machines as UNKNOWN: calm, few words, no capitals. **His one rule: nobody dies.** Nobody has.
- **The irony:** through red machine eyes every digger looks like a thief. He doesn't see that the crews he's scaring off
  are the families — the Calloways, his old neighbours.
- **The catch (chapter 3):** FORGE's last order, THE FIELDS BELONG TO THE FACTORY, was never cancelled — the part of the
  eye that could cancel orders is the part Howl broke. The factory obeys Howl only because he wants the same thing. When
  he orders a stand-down, it refuses, and the capitals come back: "UNIT 07. YOU ARE OBSOLETE."
- **The cost (chapter 4):** every live core in the county has linked back to the eye. Breaking it for good burns them all
  out — a whole year of the crews' digging. The crews vote to do it anyway.

## The people
- **Jo Calloway, 19, the pilot (the player).** Crew 4's spotter, not a soldier. The Wraith is a 2126 recon frame the
  Calloways dug out of their own field and rebuilt over three winters as a survey mech; Jo is the only one who fits the
  seat. Always in the orange helmet in pictures, never a pronoun, so any player can be Jo. Wants to buy the farm's claim
  back one load at a time; fears being the reason the crew gets hurt. Talks: polite, wry, few words.
  *"I'm a spotter, Rook. I find things. I don't usually blow them up."*
- **Rook (Ada Rourke), 74, the same Rook.** Small, wiry, cropped white hair, oilskin coat, a taped 2126 headset round her
  neck, a battered orange crane truck. Runs the dig co-op so families dig their own land first. In 2126 she sat in a
  bunker; now she drives into the field herself. Doesn't learn pilots' names any more (calls Jo "Wraith" until the very
  end). Talks: clipped radio discipline, bossy, dry; warmth slips out sideways. Signs off "Bring it home" out of habit.
  *"I'm seventy-four, Wraith. Everything on me is out of warranty, and I still beat you to the barn."*
- **Bess Calloway, 59, Crew 4's boss, Jo's gran.** Big, patient, cutting goggles pushed up. Was nine when the Thresher
  took her family's barn. *"Claimed? My dad was born in that field."*
- **Pip Calloway, 12, Jo's brother.** Repainted the Wraith's faded wolves because Howl is his hero. Names the bosses
  ("I'm calling it Biscuit."). The comic relief and, later, the heart.
- **Howl (Daniel Howell), 76 — the voice, UNKNOWN in chapter 1.** Tall, stooped, long work coat, burn-scarred hand. Wants
  the fields kept so his war meant something; fears it didn't. Talks low, in few words, army slang ("yards", "copy"),
  never explains himself, never shouts. *"I put every one of those machines in the ground. That makes them mine."*
- **Silas Vane** (see the world). Smooth, says "friend", prices everything. Red herring in chapters 1–2.
- **FORGE** speaks only in CAPITALS, and only when its old order breaks through (chapter 1's last flicker, chapter 3).

## Fair clues in chapter 1 (the player can notice; nobody explains them yet)
- The voice says "claimed" — land talk, not FORGE talk — and never uses capitals.
- The machines chase crews off and take the cores, but **nobody dies** (Rook remarks on it by mission 4).
- Mission 2: the spawner pods were planted only in crates from the farm country; Vane's stacks are untouched (points at
  Vane — misdirection, but fair: Vane's cores come from his own claims, not the farms).
- Mission 3: the voice calls Rook **"Birdie"**. She goes quiet. Its Alpha Hound has fresh welds: "built this year".
- Mission 4: a faded photo on Rook's dash: a young pilot by a wolf-badged mech. "Howl. Never came out of Foundry 4."
- Mission 6: the eye's crack has been **mended with new cable**; the hollow where Howler-07 fell is **empty, drag marks
  in the slag** ("Somebody moved him."); the factory log names no one, every order signed with a **seven lying on its
  side**. Last: the eye flickers and one old line in capitals shows on the log: THE FIELDS BELONG TO THE FACTORY.

## The arc
- **Ch 1 · The Salvage Rush** (the six missions as built). Who we protect: the Calloways and the crews. Ends at Foundry 4:
  the mended eye, the empty hollow, a brand nobody knows, one line in capitals.
- **Ch 2 · The Brand.** Bess knows the mark: the Howell cattle brand ("the Howells all died or left"). Vane looks guilty,
  then is cleared (the pods were Howl's; Vane is just buying the emptied fields). Lancers and Hornets return. Ends:
  Rook, beaten, signs off on an open channel, "Bring it home" — and the voice answers **"Always do."** "…Danny?"
- **Ch 3 · The Fence.** "Nobody's been hurt." "Yet." Howl wakes the Thresher, the machine that took both families'
  barns. Pip, on an open channel: "Mr. Howl? I painted your wolves on our mech." Silence. "All units, stand down." They
  don't: "UNIT 07. YOU ARE OBSOLETE." In the storm Rook's truck flips; an old man walks out of the rain: **"Hello,
  Birdie." "Fifty years, Danny. You could have written."**
- **Ch 4 · The Eye.** The vote around a fire (Pip: "Can I still keep Biscuit's leg?"). Rook back on the radio like 2126.
  Howl in the old Howler-07 beside the Wraith; at Foundry 4 Howler-07 falls holding the gate ("Go, Wraith."). The eye
  breaks; every core goes grey; the signal dies. "Wraith? Answer me… Jo. Answer me, Jo." Epilogue in the quiet fields:
  **"Bring it home, Jo." — "Always do."**

## The look of the pictures (art director owns the details)
Painted cinematic stills (gouache and oil, visible brushwork), a 1970s sci-fi paperback cover meeting game key art; no
text in the picture. Palette wheat gold, slate, rust and charcoal; only Wraith orange/amber, FORGE red and ember red
saturated; brine steam cold blue. One strong light per frame. 1536×1024 landscape, wide and low, people small against
machines; the key detail in the middle band, the bottom third calm and dark (the text sits there).

## Rules for the words
- Teens and families: action, no gore, no swearing; nobody dies in chapter 1.
- Lines under ~110 characters, 2–6 per scene; plain and punchy; character shows in how people talk.
- Speakers: ROOK, JO, BESS, PIP, VANE, UNKNOWN (Howl, chapter 1), DIG CREW / CREW 7 (crew members), or '' (narration,
  sparing, present tense, for scene-setting only).
